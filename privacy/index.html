Privacy Policy

November 24, 2026

Heliotide is a free app made by one person. You don't create an account with me. The app has no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no code from advertising or analytics companies. I don't sell your data.

A few features do need some data to work. This page says what it is, where it goes and how to delete it.

Who is responsible

Dima, the developer of Heliotide. For any question about your data, write to dimetry.yu@gmail.com.

Your location

The app asks for your location only while you are using it, and it asks your device for an approximate position. It uses it to show what is near you: the chance of aurora, earthquakes, fires and the weather. You can say no and choose a city instead. The app then uses that city in the same way.

Your last place is saved on your device, so the app can start there next time. It may be part of your device backup. I never receive your exact position. These parts leave your device:

  • To Apple, for weather, city names and maps. Apple Weather gets your place to return the weather. Apple's map services get it to find the name of your city, get the words you type in the city search, and load the map you look at.
  • To Apple's iCloud, for nearby alerts. If you turn notifications on, the app saves the kinds of alerts you chose, their limits and, for alerts near you, a rough square of about 110 km (70 miles) that contains your place.
  • To Apple's iCloud, for the chat. While the app is open, it asks for new messages in the room of your region, for example "Kyiv area", and in the World room. It sends a code for the room, not your position.
  • To weather services, for the rain layer. If you turn on Rain on the map, pictures of the area you are looking at come from NASA, NOAA and Environment Canada.

Notifications

Your alert choices and the rough square are kept as subscriptions in Apple's CloudKit, the iCloud service that apps use to store data, under your iCloud user for this app. Apple delivers the notifications. When you turn notifications off, the app asks iCloud to remove the subscriptions.

Chat

The chat is public. Anyone can read what you write there, so please don't post anything private. Reading needs no sign-in. Writing needs a device signed in to iCloud.

The app never shows your iCloud name or email. It shows the name you chose, or a short code like #4F2A, and the main city of your region.

These things are saved in Apple's CloudKit public database. Other people's apps can load them from there, except reports:

  • your messages, with your chat name if you set one, the room, your home region and the time; a reply also keeps a short quote;
  • your reactions;
  • which room you have open and when you were last there, to count the people online. This happens when you only read, too;
  • reports: when you hide a person from a message, a report with that message and its room is saved for the moderator. Other users can't read it.

Each of these records carries a random-looking user ID that iCloud gives you for this app. It is not your Apple Account, name or email. It is used to show your code, to let you delete your own records and to stop abuse. If you break the chat rules, your messages can be removed and that user ID can be kept in a list of people who can't write for a while.

The people you hide are kept on your device.

Feedback

When you use Send Feedback, the app saves your message, your name and email if you leave them in the form, the app version, the system version and the device model. Name and email are optional; if you have a chat name, it is filled in and you can remove it. All of this is stored in Apple's CloudKit with your iCloud user ID for this app. Other users can't read it. I use it to answer you and to fix things. If you write by email instead, your message goes to my Gmail inbox.

Where the other data comes from

Your device loads space weather, events and pictures directly from public sources such as NOAA, USGS, GDACS and NASA. Like any website, they see your IP address and ordinary technical details of the request. The app does not send them your name, your iCloud user ID or your position. The rain layer described above asks for the area on your screen.

Links that you open, in the chat or in an event, lead to websites with their own privacy rules.

What Apple does

iCloud, CloudKit, Apple Weather, Maps and notifications are run by Apple, under Apple's privacy policy. Apple may store data outside your country. Apple also gives developers App Store statistics, and crash reports from people who chose to share them in their device settings.

How long data is kept

  • Chat messages, reactions and reports stay until you delete them or they are removed by moderation. I plan to remove them automatically after 30 days. This is not running yet, so please expect them to stay longer.
  • The note of which room you last opened stays until you open a room again, which replaces it, or delete your chat data.
  • Alert subscriptions stay until you turn notifications off.
  • Feedback and emails stay until I no longer need them to help you or improve the app, or until you ask me to delete them.

How to delete your data

  • Chat: in the app, Settings, Chat, Delete my chat data. It deletes your messages, your reactions and the reports you sent. Words that others quoted from you stay in their messages. A block for breaking the rules stays too. Deleting the app alone does not delete your chat data.
  • Notifications: Settings, Notifications, turn off Notify me.
  • Location: change it at any time in your device settings, under Privacy & Security, Location Services.
  • Feedback and anything else: write to dimetry.yu@gmail.com.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you can ask for a copy of your data, ask me to correct or delete it, object to how it is used, or take back a permission you gave. Write to me and I will help. I may ask for a detail, such as your chat code, to find the right records. You can also complain to the data protection authority where you live.

I use your data only to run the features you use, with your permission for location and notifications, and to keep the chat safe, which is a legitimate interest for everyone in it.

Children

Heliotide is not directed at children under 13, and I don't knowingly collect their data. If you think a child has shared personal data in the app, write to me and I will delete it.

This website

These pages set no cookies and have no trackers. They are hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub records visitors' IP addresses for security, under the GitHub privacy statement.

Changes

If the app starts to use data in a new way, I will update this page and the date at the top.
